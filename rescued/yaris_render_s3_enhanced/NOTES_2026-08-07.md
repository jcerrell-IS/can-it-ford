## Two latent bugs in the canonical driver, not inherited

Both are live in `sim_standing.py` right now. Neither was edited there; they are simply
not reproduced here.

1. **`summary.json` can lie about bulk_modulus.** `sim_standing.py:355` writes
   `"bulk_modulus": 1.5e5` as a hardcoded literal rather than reading what the scene used.
   Harmless while the value is never varied, and silently wrong the moment it is, which is
   exactly what this work does. Here it is read from `scene.bulk_modulus`.
2. **`KeyError` after all the compute is spent.** `sim_standing.py:302` checkpoints frames
   `(0, 45, frames-1)` and `:333` reads `checkpoints["45"]` unconditionally, so any run
   with `frames <= 45` dies at the very end, after the full rollout. Here the mid
   checkpoint is `frames//2` and the save reads the same variable that wrote it.

## The login-node trap, caught before it cost anything

A recorded finding says `from warpmpm.vehicle import load_vehicle` **blocks on the Vista
login node**: 600 s wall for 0.75 s of CPU, exit 124, against 78.9 s to completion on a
compute node. Near-zero CPU over ten minutes is a blocking call, not contention.

My first version imported warpmpm at module scope, so `--estimate` would have hung on the
login node, which is the one place it must work, since its whole purpose is to cost a job
before spending SUs. Engine imports are now lazy behind `_load_engine()`, called only
after the `--estimate` early return. Verified: `--estimate` imports zero warpmpm and zero
trimesh, and runs on the Vista login node in **0.178 s**.

## Cost ladder, measured not guessed

Work is relative to the g64 / 90-frame baseline. Substeps scale **linearly** in sound
speed, which is why a physical bulk modulus is so expensive.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| g64 K=1.5e5 (as-run) | 12.85 | 11 | 2.038 | 1.00x | ~2 min |
| g96 K=1.5e5 | 12.85 | 16 | 3.057 | 4.91x | ~7 min |
| g128 K=1.5e5 | 12.85 | 21 | 4.076 | 15.27x | ~23 min |
| g64 K=1.5e7 (c 10x) | 128.45 | 104 | 2.038 | 9.45x | ~14 min |
| **g96 K=1.5e7 (hero)** | **128.45** | **156** | **3.057** | **47.86x** | **~72 min** |
| g64 K=1.99e9 (real water) | 1479.53 | 1197 | 2.038 | 108.82x | ~2.7 hr |

Real water sound speed at any useful grid is out of reach on 670 SUs: g96 at c=1481 would
be ~367x, roughly 9 hours on one node.

**Resolution reality check.** Even the hero run reaches 3.057 cells per flow depth against
a rule of thumb of ~10 particles per depth. Reaching 10 needs about n_grid 314, which is
~118x the g64 cell count before any sound-speed factor. This ladder narrows the gap; it
does not close it, and the runs must not be described as converged.

### Two validations, one of them not tuned for

**Control gate: -0.2106 percent** against the canonical g64_m1100 `final_disp_mag_m` of
0.6585370302200317. Inside the 1 percent gate.

**The grid transition reproduced the canonical study to 0.17 percentage points.** Measured
g64 -> g96 displacement change is **-59.03 percent**; CLAUDE.md item 5 records **-59.2
percent** for the canonical g64 -> g96 at 1100 kg. Nothing here was fitted to that number,
so it is independent evidence that this driver behaves identically to the canonical
pipeline when the physics is held fixed. It also reproduces the known non-monotonicity
rather than smoothing it away.

### Finding 1. P-2 pass-through is at least partly a RESOLUTION artifact

Raising g64 -> g96 with physics unchanged moves passthrough from 0.10673 (fail) to
0.09654 (pass). Seven of the 17 gated runs fail P-2 and the cause has been open. This is
the first measurement bearing on it.

**It does NOT refute the rigid-particle zero-stress hypothesis** (`mpm_utils.py:1100`
zeroes rigid stress, no `mat == 8` branch ever assigns one). Both could be true: the hull
exerting no pressure would produce pass-through, and a coarse grid would worsen it. Do not
write this up as having identified the cause.

### Finding 2. Sound speed qualitatively flips the rigid-body outcome

At FIXED grid (g96), raising bulk_modulus 1.5e5 -> 1.5e7, i.e. c 12.85 -> 128.45 m/s:

| quantity | c=12.85 | c=128.45 |
|---|---:|---:|
| displacement (m) | 0.269206 | 0.488388 (+81.4 pct) |
| **roll (deg)** | **-0.003607** | **+1.109117** (SIGN FLIP, ~300x) |
| z rise (m) | 0.000000 | -0.043675 |
| passthrough | 0.096536 | 0.085024 |
| leaked particle-frames | 153172 | 37488 (4x fewer) |

This is direct confirmation of Isik and He 2022, which CLAUDE.md records as the citable
result that artificial sound speed can qualitatively flip a rigid-body outcome. **This
project had never swept that knob.** Every one of the 17 gated runs used c = 12.845 m/s,
off from real water by a factor of 115.3.

### Finding 3. The hero run FAILS P-3, and that is not a win

`enh_g96_c10` has `C2_veh_zmin_rise = -0.043675 m`, so `abs(rise) > 0.01` and it fails
P-3. The hull sank 4.4 cm into the floor plane. That is the same pathology CLAUDE.md item
7 records for all three g48 runs ("negative z rise near -0.05 m, the hull sank into the
floor plane").

So the higher sound speed **traded a P-2 pass for a P-3 fail**. It is not straightforwardly
"more correct", and it must not be presented as the better run. The honest statement is
that both configurations violate a containment gate, in different ways.

### Verdict unchanged

All three are NO-FORD at 5.38x to 13.14x the 0.05 m DRIFT_THRESHOLD. As with the Bingham
ladder, the verdict is invariant because the margin is large, not because these knobs do
not matter: displacement moved by a factor of 2.4 across the three runs.

## Wall-clock estimates were wrong by ~30x, corrected

Predicted ~81 min for the ladder; actual job elapsed **2:09**. The error came from scaling
a historical "~1.5 min baseline" that included job setup and warp JIT warmup. Visible in
the data: g64 took 13.4 s for 11 substeps while g96 took 5.5 s for 16, i.e. the FIRST run
of a job pays the JIT cost.

Replaced with a model calibrated on the two clean g96 points (16 substeps -> 5.5 s,
156 -> 30.0 s): `wall ~= (2.7 + 0.175*substeps) * (grid/96)^3` seconds. It reproduces both
points (6 s and 30 s).

**This refuted my own warning.** The estimator previously printed ">500 substeps/frame is
not affordable on 670 SUs". That is false. Real water sound speed is reachable:
g128 at c = 1480.976 m/s is 2396 substeps, about 17 min on one GH200 node.

It is the only run in the set that combines a **physically correct sound speed** with
passing both containment gates:

- c = 1480.976 m/s, error factor **1.0000162** against real water's 1481. Every one of the

### Finding 4. Sound-speed sensitivity is NON-MONOTONE. Do not extrapolate.

At fixed grid g96, displacement against the c = 12.85 baseline:

```
c =   12.85   0.26921     (as-run value, all 17 gated runs)
c =  128.45   0.48839     +81.4 percent      <- the outlier
c = 1480.98   0.28232      +4.9 percent      <- physically correct
```

The intermediate sound speed is the outlier, not the endpoint. The artificial 12.845 m/s
happens to land within 5 percent of the physical answer at this operating point, while a
10x correction toward physical is off by 81 percent.

**Consequence, and it is the most useful methodological result here:** a partial
sound-speed correction is worse than either endpoint, and no intermediate sweep point can
be used to extrapolate toward the physical value. Anyone testing sound-speed sensitivity
must run the physical value, not a cheaper multiple of the artificial one.

This does not vindicate 12.845 m/s. The agreement is at ONE operating point, on ONE
metric, and roll still differs (-0.004 vs +0.590 deg).

### Finding 5. Grid sensitivity roughly halves at the physical sound speed

Same grid pair, g96 -> g128, at two sound speeds:

```
c =  128.45    0.48839 -> 0.67768    +38.8 percent
c = 1480.98    0.28232 -> 0.33524    +18.7 percent
```

For reference the canonical-regime g64 -> g96 at c = 12.85 is -59.03 percent.

Suggestive that the artificial sound speed amplifies grid sensitivity, which would matter
for the project's unconverged-resolution limitation. **NOT established:** this is one grid
pair at two sound speeds, with no third point and no repeat. Do not put it in the paper
without more points.

### Finding 6. Every g128 run fails P-3

`enh_g128_c10` -0.02406 and `enh_g128_real` -0.01637 both fail, while both g96 runs at the
same sound speeds pass or nearly pass. The hull sinks into the floor plane more at higher
resolution. Cause not established; recorded as an observation, not a diagnosis. It means
the highest-resolution runs are NOT the ones to quote.

### Verdict

**All six are NO-FORD**, spanning 0.26921 to 0.67768 m, i.e. 5.38x to 13.55x the 0.05 m
DRIFT_THRESHOLD. The verdict is invariant across a 115x sound-speed range, a 2x grid
refinement, and a 2.5x spread in displacement. As with the Bingham ladder, that is because
the margin is large, not because the knobs do not matter.

Three stages, **gated**: stage 0 is a control at the exact as-run baseline. If it does not

At `tau_y = 0, hb_K = 0` the enhanced driver should be arithmetically identical to
`sim_standing.py`: `eta_cap` collapses to `water_eta` exactly, and the `.with_yield` /
`.with_powerlaw` calls are skipped rather than applied with neutral arguments, so the
material object is the same one. Any control drift is therefore venue or warp-version
drift, not a code difference.

## What this does NOT fix, and must not be claimed

**The hull exerts no pressure on the water.** `mpm_utils.py:1100` initialises rigid
particle stress to a zero mat33, `:1104` excludes material 8 from the SVD, and no
`mat == 8` branch in `:1105-1147` ever assigns one. This is very likely the same defect as
the P-2 pass-through failures, but that identification has **not** been tested and must not
be asserted as fact. Fixing it means patching a vendored engine at a pinned SHA, which no
driver can do. It is the single largest realism gap remaining and this work does not
touch it.

Also unaddressed: no inflow/outflow boundary conditions exist upstream at all (zero hits
across the install), so the channel remains a Dirichlet velocity clamp on an upstream
particle slab, not a real BC. The citable design for that is Zhao, Bolognin, Liang, Rohe
and Vardon 2019, Computers and Fluids 179, 27-33, DOI 10.1016/j.compfluid.2018.10.007,
implemented in Anura3D. Writing it is a translation, not a port.
