# Drift Threshold Citation Research

## Executive finding

No direct peer-reviewed citation was found for a **0.05 m lateral vehicle drift/displacement threshold** that defines floodwater vehicle instability; the peer-reviewed vehicle-flood literature overwhelmingly defines the failure condition as **incipient motion / started to slide**, **washaway**, or **floating**, expressed as critical depth-velocity or force-balance thresholds rather than as a finite displacement in meters. The strongest defensible reframing is to state that `DRIFT_THRESHOLD = 0.05 m` is a **numerical onset-of-motion detection threshold** corresponding to about **2.5-3.4% of representative passenger/SUV vehicle body width**, using peer-reviewed vehicle-width values from Xia et al.'s Honda Accord/Audi Q7 model-prototype study and Shah et al.'s Perodua Viva model-prototype study ([Xia et al. 2014, DOI: 10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2); [Shah et al. 2018, DOI: 10.1051/matecconf/201820307003](https://www.matec-conferences.org/articles/matecconf/pdf/2018/62/matecconf_iccoee2018_07003.pdf)).

## Task 2 — Literature findings for vehicle-in-floodwater drift/displacement criteria

### Direct displacement threshold search result

No source found in this search gives a finite displacement threshold like **0.05 m**, **5 cm**, or a specified percentage of vehicle width/track width as the criterion for floodwater vehicle failure. Instead, the literature defines vehicle flood instability at the **incipient motion** condition, i.e., the hydraulic/force condition when a vehicle **starts to slide**, **is swept away**, or **floats**, rather than after a prescribed lateral displacement distance ([Shu et al. 2011, DOI: 10.1080/00221686.2011.616318](https://doi.org/10.1080/00221686.2011.616318); [Xia et al. 2014, DOI: 10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2); [Martínez-Gomariz et al. 2018, DOI: 10.1111/jfr3.12262](https://doi.org/10.1111/jfr3.12262)).

### Physical bound for reframing `DRIFT_THRESHOLD = 0.05 m`

The most defensible percentage-based reframing is **vehicle body width**, because flood-vehicle stability studies explicitly define and report vehicle width as part of the model/prototype geometry used in the stability equations and experiments. Xia et al. state that “The vehicle dimensions are characterized by the length lc, width bc, and roof height hc,” and their Table 1 gives prototype widths of **1.845 m** for a Honda Accord and **1.983 m** for an Audi Q7 ([Xia et al. 2014, DOI: 10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2); [Cardiff ORCA PDF](https://orca.cardiff.ac.uk/id/eprint/54002/1/Xia%202014.pdf)). Shah et al. report a Perodua Viva prototype width of **1475 mm = 1.475 m** in Table 1, and Smith et al. report a Toyota Yaris width of **1.69 m** and Nissan Patrol width of **1.84 m** in Table 5-1 ([Shah et al. 2018, DOI: 10.1051/matecconf/201820307003](https://www.matec-conferences.org/articles/matecconf/pdf/2018/62/matecconf_iccoee2018_07003.pdf); [Smith et al. 2017 WRL Technical Report 2017/07](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/engineering/civil-environmental/water-research-laboratory/publications/WRL-TR2017-07-Vehicle-Stability-Testing-for-Flood-Flows.pdf)).

Ground clearance is a weaker normalization for `0.05 m` because the threshold becomes a large fraction of clearance rather than a small lateral-motion detector. For example, Smith et al.'s full-scale Toyota Yaris floorpan height is **0.155 m**, the Australian vehicle-body clearance minimum cited in that report is **100 mm**, and the ARR-style small-passenger ground-clearance class in the same report is **< 0.12 m**, making 0.05 m equal to **32.3%**, **50.0%**, or **41.7%** of those vertical dimensions, respectively ([Smith et al. 2017 WRL Technical Report 2017/07](https://www.unsw.edu.au/content/dam/pdfs/engineering/civil-environmental/water-research-laboratory/publications/WRL-TR2017-07-Vehicle-Stability-Testing-for-Flood-Flows.pdf)).

## Recommendation for the L2 `DRIFT_THRESHOLD`

Use recommendation **(b)**, not **(a)**: no direct literature-backed **0.05 m floodwater drift threshold** was found, so do not present 0.05 m as a published vehicle-stability criterion. Instead, write it as: **“The L2 simulation uses 0.05 m lateral displacement as a conservative numerical onset-of-motion detector, corresponding to roughly 2.5-3.4% of representative passenger/SUV vehicle body width; flood-vehicle stability literature defines failure by incipient motion/started-to-slide rather than by a prescribed finite drift distance.”** This phrasing is supported by Xia et al.'s use of “started to slide” as the experimental threshold event, by Shu et al.'s depth-velocity “threshold of vehicle instability,” and by representative vehicle widths from Xia et al. and Shah et al. ([Shu et al. 2011, DOI: 10.1080/00221686.2011.616318](https://doi.org/10.1080/00221686.2011.616318); [Xia et al. 2014, DOI: 10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2); [Shah et al. 2018, DOI: 10.1051/matecconf/201820307003](https://www.matec-conferences.org/articles/matecconf/pdf/2018/62/matecconf_iccoee2018_07003.pdf)).

Suggested paper/poster wording:

> Because published flood-vehicle stability studies typically define failure at incipient motion—e.g., recording the hydraulic state when the flooded vehicle “started to slide”—rather than at a prescribed finite displacement, the L2 model treats 0.05 m lateral displacement as a conservative numerical onset-of-motion detector. This equals about 2.5-3.4% of representative vehicle body width across the Audi Q7/Honda Accord/Perodua Viva geometries reported in vehicle-flood stability experiments, so the threshold is better interpreted as a simulation decision tolerance for detecting incipient sliding, not as a published flood-hazard scalar or empirically validated displacement limit ([Xia et al. 2014, DOI: 10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11069-013-0889-2); [Shah et al. 2018, DOI: 10.1051/matecconf/201820307003](https://www.matec-conferences.org/articles/matecconf/pdf/2018/62/matecconf_iccoee2018_07003.pdf)).

**Takeaway for Josie:** use 0.02 as the best-supported PhysGaussian implementation threshold, but cite it as an official-code default rather than a paper-stated value ([PhysGaussian `decode_param.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/utils/decode_param.py#L121-L126)).

## Section 2: Public code bridge status

### 2.1 Official PhysGaussian implementation

The official PhysGaussian repository is `XPandora/PhysGaussian`, and its project page describes the method as treating Gaussian ellipsoids as MPM particle discretization, followed by internal filling for better physics compliance ([XPandora/PhysGaussian](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian), [PhysGaussian project page](https://xpandora.github.io/PhysGaussian/)). The official repository contains the preprocessing bridge pieces requested here: opacity filtering in `gs_simulation.py`, default opacity threshold parsing in `utils/decode_param.py`, coordinate transforms in `utils/transformation_utils.py`, cuboid `sim_area` filtering in `gs_simulation.py`, and internal filling in `particle_filling/filling.py` ([PhysGaussian `gs_simulation.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/gs_simulation.py#L122-L173), [PhysGaussian `decode_param.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/utils/decode_param.py#L121-L141), [PhysGaussian `transformation_utils.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/utils/transformation_utils.py), [PhysGaussian `filling.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/particle_filling/filling.py)).

The official implementation is not a Genesis implementation; it imports `MPM_Simulator_WARP` from its own `mpm_solver_warp` package and the README links its solver lineage to `zeshunzong/warp-mpm` ([PhysGaussian `gs_simulation.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/gs_simulation.py#L17), [zeshunzong/warp-mpm](https://github.com/zeshunzong/warp-mpm), [XPandora/PhysGaussian README](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian)). The official demos/configs target objects and materials such as jelly, sand, metal, foam, snow, plasticine, ficus, plane, wolf, pillow/sofa, bread, and vase/deck examples, not a road/water/vehicle flood scene ([XPandora/PhysGaussian README](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian), [PhysGaussian project page](https://xpandora.github.io/PhysGaussian/)).

### 2.2 Genesis-specific status

The current Genesis repository is `Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world`, and the Genesis documentation lists MPM materials including `Elastic`, `ElastoPlastic`, `Liquid`, `Muscle`, `Sand`, and `Snow` ([Genesis repository](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world), [Genesis MPM materials docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_reference/material/mpm/index.html)). The Genesis `MPM.Liquid` API exposes density `rho`, elastic parameters, and a `viscous` flag, and the “Beyond Rigid Bodies” tutorial shows a liquid MPM box and an MPM simulation domain with `lower_bound`, `upper_bound`, and `visualize_mpm_boundary` options ([Genesis `MPM.Liquid` docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_reference/material/mpm/liquid.html), [Genesis Beyond Rigid Bodies tutorial](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/getting_started/beyond_rigid_bodies.html)).

No exact public PhysGaussian-to-Genesis bridge was found in the Genesis repository, Genesis examples, or Genesis issue/discussion search evidence gathered for this task; the relevant Genesis examples are general MPM and coupling examples such as `examples/tutorials/mpm.py`, `examples/coupling/water_wheel.py`, `examples/coupling/sph_mpm.py`, and `examples/coupling/rigid_mpm_attachment.py`, not 3DGS-kernel preprocessing bridges ([Genesis repository](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world), [Genesis MPM materials docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_reference/material/mpm/index.html)). The closest Genesis issue evidence was a feature request to port an OmniPhysGS MPM result into Genesis, which is not a completed PhysGaussian bridge and does not provide the road/water/vehicle flood pipeline ([Genesis issue #965](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world/issues/965)).

### 2.3 Related Gaussian-to-Genesis work that is not the exact bridge

GASP is public code that integrates a Gaussian-splatting-based representation with Genesis, and its paper states that it demonstrates “the integration of a GS-based model with the Genesis engine” ([GASP paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.05819v3), [GASP repository](https://github.com/waczjoan/GASP)). GASP is not the exact PhysGaussian bridge because it uses GaMeS flat Gaussians/pseudomesh ideas and a Genesis patch workflow rather than PhysGaussian’s original-kernel opacity filtering plus internal opacity-field filling; its README instructs users to clone Genesis, checkout a specific Genesis commit, apply `genesis_examples/genesis_gasp.patch`, and run a bottle example with mesh input ([GASP repository](https://github.com/waczjoan/GASP)).

A separate public repository, `ranrandy/gaussian-splatting-mpm`, appears to be a Taichi-oriented reimplementation inspired by PhysGaussian, but it is not a Genesis `MPM.Liquid` road/water/vehicle bridge ([ranrandy/gaussian-splatting-mpm](https://github.com/ranrandy/gaussian-splatting-mpm)). This repository may be useful as a small-code reference for gsplat-to-MPM concepts, but it should not be presented as an existing Genesis flood-scene implementation ([ranrandy/gaussian-splatting-mpm](https://github.com/ranrandy/gaussian-splatting-mpm)).

### 2.4 Hsiao and Kumar arXiv:2507.09005 assessment

Hsiao and Kumar’s paper, “From images to properties: a NeRF-driven framework for granular material parameter inversion,” is arXiv:2507.09005 with DOI `10.48550/arXiv.2507.09005`, and it reconstructs geometry from initial multi-view images using NeRF before initializing material points for MPM and using Bayesian optimization to infer a granular friction angle ([Hsiao and Kumar arXiv:2507.09005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09005), [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09005v1)). The paper is highly relevant to Josie’s mentor context because it is a reconstruct-to-MPM bridge, but it is not the same as PhysGaussian’s 3DGS-kernel bridge: it uses NeRF geometry, granular material property inversion, and MPM material-point sampling from reconstructed geometry rather than 3D Gaussian opacity filtering and opacity-field internal filling ([Hsiao and Kumar arXiv:2507.09005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09005), [arXiv HTML v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09005v1)).

No formal public code release for arXiv:2507.09005 was identified in the paper evidence gathered here, and the related `chhsiao93/mpm-sandbox` repository is a Taichi-MPM sandbox for sand/NeRF-style experiments rather than a Genesis or 3DGS-to-MPM.Liquid bridge ([chhsiao93/mpm-sandbox](https://github.com/chhsiao93/mpm-sandbox)). The sandbox can inform sampling and inverse-material thinking, but it does not solve the road/water/vehicle Genesis port that Josie needs ([chhsiao93/mpm-sandbox](https://github.com/chhsiao93/mpm-sandbox), [Hsiao and Kumar arXiv:2507.09005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.09005)).

## Section 3: Recommendation for Josie

### 3.1 What to adapt

Josie should adapt the official PhysGaussian preprocessing code, not its solver, because the preprocessing code already implements the missing gsplat-to-particle steps and the solver backend is not Genesis ([XPandora/PhysGaussian](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian), [PhysGaussian `gs_simulation.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/gs_simulation.py#L17), [PhysGaussian `filling.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/particle_filling/filling.py)). The minimum adapter should read the trained Gaussian `.ply`, apply the 0.02 opacity mask, apply manual rotation and `sim_area` crop, normalize/shift into a target Genesis coordinate frame, run optional internal filling, and export particle arrays such as `pos`, `volume`, `mass`, `material_tag`, and optional visual attributes for a Genesis entity or custom particle initialization path ([PhysGaussian `gs_simulation.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/gs_simulation.py#L122-L173), [PhysGaussian `filling.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/particle_filling/filling.py), [Genesis `MPM.Liquid` docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_reference/material/mpm/liquid.html)).

For the “Can It Ford?” pipeline, the bridge should treat gsplat reconstruction as the **Perceive** stage, PhysGaussian-style particle generation as the **Predict initialization** stage, Genesis `MPM.Liquid` plus rigid vehicle coupling as the **Predict simulation** stage, ford/no-ford verdict logic as the **Control query** stage, and L0/L1/L2 comparison plus uncertainty checks as the **Guarantee** stage ([can-it-ford project context](file:///home/user/workspace/skills/space/can-it-ford-reu/references/project-context.md), [Genesis Beyond Rigid Bodies tutorial](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/getting_started/beyond_rigid_bodies.html)). This framing makes the missing public bridge a legitimate project contribution rather than a simple dependency lookup ([XPandora/PhysGaussian](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian), [Genesis repository](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world)).

### 3.2 What to build from scratch

The Genesis ingestion layer should be built from scratch because no public implementation was found that takes PhysGaussian-filled particles and initializes a Genesis `MPM.Liquid` road/water/vehicle scene ([Genesis repository](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/genesis-world), [Genesis `MPM.Liquid` docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_reference/material/mpm/liquid.html)). The road/flood-specific design should define a metric simulation box, such as the project’s road channel dimensions, remap the PhysGaussian normalized coordinates into that metric box, and assign water/road/vehicle material semantics explicitly rather than assuming the object-demo material presets in PhysGaussian ([can-it-ford project context](file:///home/user/workspace/skills/space/can-it-ford-reu/references/project-context.md), [XPandora/PhysGaussian README](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian)).

The most direct initial deliverable should be a standalone `physgaussian_to_genesis_particles.py` script that outputs an `.npz` file with particle positions and metadata, followed by a Genesis script that loads those arrays and compares a baseline box-water initialization against the reconstructed-particle initialization ([PhysGaussian `filling.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/particle_filling/filling.py), [Genesis Beyond Rigid Bodies tutorial](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user_guide/getting_started/beyond_rigid_bodies.html)). The first validation should be geometric rather than dynamic: compare particle counts, bounding boxes, voxel occupancy, and free-surface height before running the full vehicle-coupled L2 experiment ([PhysGaussian `filling.py`](https://github.com/XPandora/PhysGaussian/blob/8339ed6aa2cd5d50e1001a254a3d95aea678a956/particle_filling/filling.py), [Genesis MPM materials docs](https://genesis-world.readthedocs.io/en/latest/api_reference/material/mpm/index.html)).
