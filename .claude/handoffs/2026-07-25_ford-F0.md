## The figure

Depth on x, velocity on y, all 70 cells drawn as rectangles centered on their sample points in
true physical coordinates, so the cap lines are geometrically correct rather than drawn on a
categorical grid. Fill encodes how many of the three classes are cleared: 46 cells none,
5 cells Large 4WD only, 7 cells Large 4WD plus large passenger, 12 cells all three. The two
middle categories are exactly the 12 class-sensitive cells; they carry a diagonal hatch and a
heavy outline on top of fill, so they read as a single band and survive grayscale printing.

Caps drawn as required: three vertical lines at depth 0.30 / 0.40 / 0.50 m, and three
hyperbolas v = cap / d for D x V 0.30 / 0.45 / 0.60 m2/s, color and dash coded per class, each
with a white halo so they stay legible where they cross the darkest fill.

Poster constraints, all met:

## Finding for whoever owns the L1 code: four cells are decided by floating point, not by AR&R

This does not change any of the four numbers I was told to verify. All four match. But it does
change which cells are hatched, so flagging it before this goes to print.

`vehicle_params.py:197` compares the raw IEEE-754 double `depth_m * velocity_ms` against the
class hazard cap. Commit 63e677f describes the DV bound as inclusive. It is inclusive in
intent, but four cells whose D x V is mathematically exactly on a cap produce a double one ULP
above it and return NO-FORD:

| depth | velocity | class | cap | double product |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 3.0 | small_passenger | 0.30 | 0.30000000000000004 |
| 0.2 | 1.5 | small_passenger | 0.30 | 0.30000000000000004 |
| 0.2 | 3.0 | large_4wd | 0.60 | 0.6000000000000001 |
| 0.4 | 1.5 | large_4wd | 0.60 | 0.6000000000000001 |

The inconsistency is self-evident inside the shipped data: at depth 0.3, velocity 1.0 the
product is exactly 0.3 as a double, so that cell returns FORD while (0.2, 1.5) with the same
mathematical D x V returns NO-FORD. The CSV makes it harder to spot, because `L1_haz` is
`round(d*v, 4)` and therefore prints 0.3 or 0.6 for all of them, reading as inside the cap
while the verdict column says outside.

Impact if the comparison were made tolerant, for example `round(d*v, 9)`:
- small_passenger FORD goes 12 to 14
- large_4wd FORD goes 24 to 26
- large_passenger stays 19
- class-sensitive stays 12, but two members swap: (0.1, 3.0) and (0.2, 1.5) leave the set,
  (0.2, 3.0) and (0.4, 1.5) enter it

So two of the twelve hatched cells on the poster would be different cells. The scientific
claim, that vehicle class flips the verdict in 12 of 70 scenarios, survives unchanged either
way, which is why I plotted the shipped data rather than stopping.
