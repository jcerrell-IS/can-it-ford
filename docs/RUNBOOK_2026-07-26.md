## 3. Stage B: the parameter sweeps

This is the "multiple simulations across parameters" part. Two sweeps, designed so that
one of them is completely free of discretization confounds.

### 3.1 Why the velocity sweep is the good one

`sim_standing.py:82` sets the domain from the vehicle:

```python
lim = float(max(2.2 * ext[1], 3.5 * ext[0], 6.0 * depth))
```

With the Yaris and depth 0.30 the vehicle term wins, so `lim = 9.421742` is fixed. Then
`dx = lim/n_grid`, `h = dx/2`, and the water column is
`arange(floor + 0.5h, floor + depth, h)`.

Hold depth at 0.30 and vary only velocity, and **every discretization quantity stays
bit-identical**: same `lim`, same `dx`, same `h`, same 4 layers, same 48367 water
particles. Even the timestep is unchanged, because `substeps` is acoustic-limited:

```
term_acoustic  = c / (0.28 dx) = 12.84523 / 0.0412202 = 311.6253
term_advective = v / (0.5 dx)  = 3.0 / 0.0736074      =  40.757   at the sweep's fastest case
substeps = ceil(311.6253 / 30) = 11    for every velocity in the sweep
```

So the velocity sweep is a clean single-variable experiment. Nothing else moves.

### 3.2 Why the depth sweep is not, and what to say about it

Depth changes the layer count:

| n_grid | h (m) | d=0.15 | 0.20 | 0.25 | 0.30 | 0.35 | 0.45 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 48 | 0.098143 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
| 64 | 0.073607 | 2 | 3 | 3 | **4** | 5 | 6 |
| 96 | 0.049072 | 3 | 4 | 5 | **6** | 7 | 9 |
| 128 | 0.036804 | 4 | 5 | 7 | 8 | 10 | 12 |

A depth sweep at g64 therefore compares a 3-layer column against a 6-layer column. That is
a real result but it conflates depth with water-column resolution. Run it, and state the
confound in the caption. Do not run depths below 0.25 at g64: two layers is not a fluid.

Two of the three masses converge **oscillatorily**: displacement rises from g48 to g64, then
falls at g96. A negative ratio has no real logarithm, so the observed order is undefined and
Richardson extrapolation is invalid. The one monotonic case gives an observed order of
**0.149**, far below any theoretical order for this scheme, which says the solution is
nowhere near the asymptotic range either.

Also note the refinement ratios are not uniform: `h48/h64 = 1.3333` but `h64/h96 = 1.5000`.
Even with monotonic data that non-uniformity requires the transcendental form of the GCI,
not the textbook one.

**What survives, and what does not.**

- **Does not survive:** any quoted displacement magnitude. The poster's headline
  `|d| = 0.6585 m` at g64/1100 kg is the least converged point in the entire set. At g96 the
  same case gives 0.2689 m, a factor of 2.4 smaller. Do not put a converged-looking number
  on the poster.
- **Survives:** the verdict. `DRIFT_THRESHOLD_M = 0.05`. Every one of the nine runs finishes
  between 0.0855 m and 0.6585 m, all of them above the threshold by at least 1.7x. **L2 is
  NO-FORD in all nine runs, at every grid, for every mass.** The binary result is
  grid-independent even though its magnitude is not.
- **Also survives:** the mass ordering. At every grid, heavier displaces less
  (1100 > 1609 > 2337). That trend is monotonic in mass at all three resolutions.
- `passthrough_max_frac` falls with refinement at every mass, which is the expected direction
  and mild independent evidence the refinement is doing what it should.

The honest poster sentence is that the vehicle moves far enough to fail the stability
criterion at every resolution tested, while the displacement magnitude is not yet
grid-converged and is therefore reported as a bound rather than a value.

### 5.5 If you want real vehicle classes

`sim_standing.py` exposes `--vehicle`, so a scaled mesh needs no code edit. But scaling the
mesh changes `ext`, which changes `lim`, which changes `dx`, `h`, and the layer count. A
previous session measured exactly this:

| class | length scale | lim | dx | h | layers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| small_passenger | 1.00000 | 9.42174 | 0.147215 | 0.073607 | 4 |
| large_passenger | 1.14416 | 10.78000 | 0.168438 | 0.084219 | 4 |
| large_4wd | 1.21421 | 11.44000 | 0.178750 | 0.089375 | **3** |

The 4WD row discretizes the water column 25 percent coarser, so any displacement difference

## 7. Stage F: photoreal render

### 7.1 What it does and what it costs

`render_realistic.py` builds a marching-cubes isosurface of the water and composites it with
a three-material shaded Yaris mesh. Measured on this Mac just now, frame 45 of `g64_m1100`:

```
3.7 s wall, watertight True, enclosed volume 19.755 m3 against 19.289 true (+2.4 percent)
50000 car faces segmented into 36631 body, 6542 glass, 6827 tires
244652 water faces total, 132006 drawn after view-box crop
```

Running a sequence amortizes the imports, so it is faster still. Measured over frames 44 to
46 via `render_seq.py`: 5.2 s wall for 3 frames, about **1.7 s per frame**. A 90-frame
sequence is therefore about **2.5 minutes**, not hours. This is cheap. Run it.

### 7.2 The surfacing parameters, and why they are what they are

Do not change these casually, each was calibrated:

- `cell = h/2`. Measured 1.068 particles per occupied voxel. Using `cell = h` instead gives
  **+41.24 percent** volume error and its full isolevel sweep never enters the 5 percent
  band. `h/2` gives roughly 2.4 percent.
- **4-cell pad**, not Kumar's hardcoded `0.012 m`. On the 0.4 m dough domain 0.012 m is
  3.6 cells; on this 9.42 m flood domain it is 0.326 cells, narrower than the `sigma=1.3`
  blur. Kumar's function verbatim returns an **open** mesh here: `watertight False`,
  `euler 551`, volume 2.63 m3 against 19.29. No isolevel fixes an open mesh.
- `ISO_FRAC = 0.90`, adaptive relative to the mean nonzero density. Calibrated on frame 45.
- `sigma = 1.3`.

### 7.3 Known defect: matplotlib depth sorting

Look at any output frame and you will see the water sheet painted across the car's doors,
windscreen, and roofline, well above the true waterline. The measured waterline is
**21.9 percent of car height** (water surface 0.736074, car spans 0.402288 to 1.923016),
which is just above the wheel centers. The render shows it much higher.

Cause: matplotlib's 3D backend is a painter's algorithm that depth-sorts each polygon by a
single averaged z. With 132006 water faces and 50000 car faces, many water polygons that
are genuinely behind the car sort in front of it. Merging everything into one
`Poly3DCollection` (which the script already does) improves this but cannot fix it, because
the sort key itself is wrong.

This is a **rendering artifact, not a physics result.** Do not describe the apparent
waterline in any caption. If a reviewer asks how deep the water is on the car, the answer is
21.9 percent of height, measured, not what the picture appears to show.

Two mitigations, in order of cost:

1. Cull water faces whose centroid lies inside the car hull. Those faces are the P-2
   penetration artifact anyway, so removing them is defensible and it removes the worst of
   the bleed-through. Section 7.5.
2. Move to a real renderer with a z-buffer. Out of scope before the poster.

### 7.4 Frame sequence for a movie

`render_realistic.py` only renders one frame per call. `render_seq.py` loops it.

**This file already exists**, created and smoke-tested on 2026-07-26 against frames 44 to 46
of `g64_m1100`: three frames, all watertight, volume errors +2.55, +2.42, +2.32 percent.
Skip to the run command below. The source is reproduced here only so the runbook is
self-contained if the file is ever lost. Do not paste the heredoc over a working copy
without diffing first.

```bash
cat > /Users/josie/can-it-ford/renders/yaris_render_s1/render_seq.py <<'PY'
from __future__ import annotations

import argparse
import json
from pathlib import Path

import render_realistic as RR


def main():
    p = argparse.ArgumentParser()
    p.add_argument("--run", required=True)
    p.add_argument("--outdir", required=True)
    p.add_argument("--start", type=int, default=0)
    p.add_argument("--stop", type=int, default=90)
    p.add_argument("--stride", type=int, default=1)
    p.add_argument("--half", type=float, default=3.0)
    p.add_argument("--dpi", type=int, default=140)
    p.add_argument("--elev", type=float, default=17.0)
    p.add_argument("--azim", type=float, default=-62.0)
    a = p.parse_args()

    out = Path(a.outdir)
    (out / "_frames").mkdir(parents=True, exist_ok=True)

    rows = []
    for f in range(a.start, a.stop, a.stride):
        dst = out / "_frames" / ("f_%04d.png" % f)
        r = RR.render(a.run, f, out=str(dst), half=a.half, dpi=a.dpi,
                      elev=a.elev, azim=a.azim)
        rows.append({"frame": f, "vol": float(r["vol"]),
                     "v_true": float(r["v_true"]),
                     "err_pct": 100.0 * (r["vol"] - r["v_true"]) / r["v_true"],
                     "watertight": bool(r["watertight"])})
        print("frame %4d  vol %8.4f  err %+6.2f%%  watertight %s"
              % (f, r["vol"], rows[-1]["err_pct"], r["watertight"]), flush=True)

    (out / "volume_audit.json").write_text(json.dumps(rows, indent=2))
    bad = [r for r in rows if not r["watertight"]]
    worst = max(abs(r["err_pct"]) for r in rows)
    print("WROTE %d frames, worst volume error %.2f%%, non-watertight frames %d"
          % (len(rows), worst, len(bad)))


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
PY
```

Run it:

```bash

```

## 11. Limitations to carry into every caption

Write these down now while they are cheap. Each is measured, not asserted.

0. **The displacement magnitude is not grid-converged.** Across n_grid 48, 64, 96 the final
   displacement converges oscillatorily for two of three masses, so the Roache GCI is
   undefined; the one monotonic case gives an observed order of 0.149, far outside the
   asymptotic range. The g64 value of 0.6585 m at 1100 kg is 2.4x the g96 value of 0.2689 m.
   Report displacement as a bound, never as a converged value. The **verdict** is unaffected:
   all nine runs exceed the 0.05 m threshold, so L2 is NO-FORD at every resolution.
1. Water column is 4 particles deep at `n_grid=64`, and only 3 at `n_grid=48`. Surface detail
   below about 0.15 m is discretization, not physics.
2. `realized_rho` is 309.74 at 1100 kg, above the 100 to 300 plausibility ceiling. The 1609
   and 2337 kg cases are a mass sensitivity study on fixed geometry, not three vehicle
   classes.
3. About 2.5 percent of water particles are boundary-projected per frame. The projection is
   non-conservative.
4. `passthrough_max_frac` 0.10670 is dominated by bounding-box void. Voxel occupancy against
   the integrated body peaks at 1.44 percent.
5. One column of water particles, 1.81 percent, is dropped by a float64-exact `arange`
   boundary. Unfixed, because fixing it changes the physics.
6. The apparent waterline in the photoreal render is a matplotlib depth-sort artifact. The
   measured waterline is 21.9 percent of car height.
7. The car mesh's lowest point sits 0.0394 m below the floor plane, close to
   `h/2 = 0.0368`, which is the voxel-center convention placing the mesh surface half a cell
   under the lowest rigid particle.
8. The acoustic round trip from vehicle to downstream wall and back is 0.49510 s over a
   3.17984 m path at 12.84523 m/s, so a 3.0 s run contains about 6.06 reflections. The
   domain is not acoustically transparent.
9. Bulk modulus is softened to 150000 Pa, roughly 1/15000 of water's true value. Density
   fluctuation scales as Ma^2, about 1.36 percent here. Whether that is acceptable for this
   claim is a judgment, not a sourced fact.
10. AR&R figures are the report's own DRAFT INTERIM values for **stationary** vehicles, not
    an endorsed safety standard. Say "draft interim" every time.
11. Dry start and standing water differ in at least six ways beyond initial condition. They
    are not a controlled comparison and no causal claim about the initial condition
    survives review.
