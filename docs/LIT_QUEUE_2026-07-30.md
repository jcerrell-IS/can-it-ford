## 2. NEEDS_LITERATURE, blocking

**Does any peer-reviewed flood-vehicle stability source define instability by an absolute
lateral drift distance near 0.05 m?**

**Exact claim text under audit**, same tex line 125:

> "Threshold currently 0.05 m with no independent citation"

**What is needed:** a yes or no across the flood-vehicle stability literature, in particular:
- Xia et al. 2014 (incipient velocity of vehicles in floodwater)
- Shah et al. 2018, *Instability Criteria for Vehicles in Motion Exposed to Flood Risks*
- Azhar, Pauwels and Bui 2023, DOI `10.1111/jfr3.12885`
- Xiong et al. 2024, DOI `10.1029/2023WR036739`
- Shand, Cox, Blacka and Smith 2011, AR&R Project 10 Stage 2, P10/S2/020

expected and useful answer: it converts the 0.05 m figure from "uncited threshold" into
"internal numerical onset-of-motion detector", which is defensible and citable to Xia and
Shah for the underlying sliding physics rather than for the number.

## 3. NEEDS_LITERATURE, newly found, high value

**What depth does the National Weather Service actually associate with vehicles being carried
away, and is 0.15 m ever a vehicle figure?**

**Exact claim text under audit**, `overleaf/main:conference_101719_1.tex` line 123, Table I:

> "L0 | Depth | Fixed depth cutoff (approx 0.15 m [nws_tadd]), independent of velocity or
> vehicle class."

**What the cited page actually says**, fetched live 2026-07-30, HTTP 200, from
`https://www.weather.gov/safety/flood-turn-around-dont-drown`:

> "A mere 6 inches of fast-moving flood water can knock over an adult. It takes just 12 inches
> of rushing water to carry away most cars and just 2 feet of rushing water can carry away
> SUVs and trucks."

6 inches = 0.1524 m and is about **an adult pedestrian**. 12 inches = 0.3048 m and is the
**vehicle** figure. The paper's vehicle depth cutoff cites the pedestrian number.

**What is needed:**
1. Confirm whether NWS, FHWA, FEMA, USGS, or the CDC publish a vehicle-specific depth
   threshold at or near 0.15 m anywhere, or whether 0.30 m (12 in) is the only vehicle figure.
2. Identify the primary source behind the NWS "12 inches" claim, if one exists.
3. Report whether the widely-repeated "6 inches / 12 inches / 2 feet" trio traces to a study
   or is a public-safety heuristic.

**Why it matters:** the paper's claim that L0 is "markedly over-conservative" (line 84) is
partly an artifact of the threshold choice. At 0.30 m, L0 would permit 21 of the 70 sweep
scenarios instead of 7. Either the threshold or the framing needs to move.
